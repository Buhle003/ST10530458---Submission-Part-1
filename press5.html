Kliptown Chess Club Celebrates Over A Decade Of Changing Young Lives

Johannesburg | June 2026

For more than a decade, the Khulisa Social Solutions’ Kliptown Chess Club has quietly been transforming lives.

Established over 10 years ago, the club has touched the lives of more than 200 children and young people, providing a safe space where discipline, strategic thinking and hope flourish. Today, the programme stands as one of Kliptown’s most important community assets, equipping young people not only to play chess, but to navigate life itself.

These were among the findings emerging from a recent Dialogue Circle facilitated with members of the club, where young people reflected on the impact chess has had on their lives and futures.

The message from participants was clear: chess is not simply a sport – it is a pathway to possibility.

Young people described chess as teaching them to think before acting, manage emotions, recover from mistakes and make better life choices. They linked the game directly to improved concentration at school, stronger family relationships and greater confidence in themselves.

“Chess teaches us that one mistake does not mean the game is over; you can still correct yourself and come back stronger.”

The Dialogue Circle revealed that in a community where many young people face daily challenges including poverty, gangs, substance abuse and limited opportunities, chess offers something profoundly different – structure, belonging and hope.

Participants reported that chess: · Improves concentration, discipline and academic performance; · Encourages responsible decision-making and emotional control; · Provides a positive alternative to gangs, smoking and negative influences; · Builds confidence, leadership and social connections; · Strengthens trust and pride within families; and · Opens young people’s minds to opportunities beyond their immediate environment.

Many participants spoke of dreams they had never imagined before joining the club: travelling, competing in tournaments, becoming coaches and inspiring younger generations.

One of the most striking findings was the role chess plays in crime prevention and social cohesion. Young people described the club as a safe space that directly competes with harmful influences by offering meaningful activities, mentorship, and a sense of belonging.

The Dialogue Circle also highlighted the club’s broader role in the community. Young people see themselves not merely as players, but as future leaders and role models who can give back to Kliptown and help others avoid the pitfalls they face every day.

“If chess disappeared,” one participant reflected, “the community would lose future stars, future coaches and future grandmasters.”

The findings underscore the importance of protecting and expanding programmes like the Khulisa Kliptown Chess Club. Beyond producing skilled players, the club is nurturing resilient young citizens who are learning to lead with patience, strategy and purpose.

As the club celebrates more than a decade of impact and over 200 young lives touched, the message from Kliptown is a powerful one: “Investing in young people is not only about changing individual lives – it is about transforming communities”.
