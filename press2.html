Khulisa Report Reveals A Shared Pattern Of Systemic Challenges Across Four Western Cape Communities

Johannesburg | August 2026

Khulisa Social Solutions released a new evidence report revealing that four different Western Cape Communities are experiencing remarkable similar social and developmental challenges, despite their different histories, demographics and economic circumstances.

The report draws on evidence gathered during July 2026 through 201 Dialogue Circles and direct engagement with young people, parents, community leaders, NGOs and other stakeholders in Cape Town CBD, Mitchell’s Plain, Gugulethu (Fezeka), and Somerset West & Strand.

While each community has its own unique identity, the evidence consistently points to the same underlying issues. Youth unemployment, poverty, substance abuse, crime, violence and fragmented support systems emerged repeatedly as the factors preventing communities from achieving their full potential.

The report’s most significant finding, however, is not simply the challenges communities face. It is that every community already possesses substantial strengths. Schools, NGOs, faith-based organisations, volunteers and local leaders are actively supporting residents every day. The greatest barrier is that these efforts are often disconnected, limiting their collective impact.

According to the report, youth unemployment emerged as the strongest and most consistent concern across all four communities. Residents repeatedly linked the lack of employment opportunities to substance abuse, crime, school dropout, poor mental health, family instability and long-term poverty.

Lesley Ann van Selm, Founder and Managing Director of Khulisa Social Solutions, said the findings demonstrate the importance of strengthening collaboration rather than creating entirely new systems.

“Communities have repeatedly shown that they are resilient and resourceful. The opportunity now is to connect existing organisations, services and community assets more effectively so that they reinforce one another and create greater collective impact.”

The report recommends a collaborative approach built around stronger coordination, shared community intelligence, improved referral pathways, strengthened NGO ecosystems and clearer pathways that connect young people to education, skills development and employment.

Khulisa hopes the findings will contribute to constructive dialogue across government, municipalities, civil society, business and communities on practical ways to strengthen social cohesion and improve outcomes for vulnerable communities.
